Tree Care and Maintenance Guide

Routine monitoring can help spot problems before they become serious. Inspect trees from the ground and use a qualified tree professional for difficult or hazardous evaluations.

Regular Inspection Points:

  • Dead, broken or hanging branches
  • Cracks, cavities, decay and unusual lean
  • Root exposure and soil movement
  • Signs of pests, disease or canopy decline
  • Branches contacting buildings or utilities

Storm Damage Response

After severe weather, avoid unstable branches, fallen trees and downed lines. Photograph damage from a safe distance where useful, and seek a professional risk assessment before attempting cleanup.

The Tree Removal Procedure

The typical steps below are a general overview; exact procedures will vary by tree, property and contractor.

Step 1: Assessment & Quote

  • Review the tree and site conditions

Step 2: Pre-Removal Planning

  • Arrange equipment positioning

Step 3: Professional Removal Process

For standard removals: Crews may remove branches and sections of trunk using controlled cutting and lowering techniques.

For emergency situations: Priorities are hazard assessment, area safety, utility coordination and an appropriate removal plan.

Step 4: Cleanup & Completion

Seasonal Tree Trimming Guidelines

The best pruning time depends on tree species, health and the reason for trimming. Emergency hazard reduction can take priority at any time of year.

Spring (March – May)

Inspect for winter damage, broken branches and new growth. Avoid unnecessary pruning that may stress vulnerable trees.

Summer (June – August)

Consider light corrective pruning as needed. In North Texas, ask a qualified professional about oak-wilt prevention before pruning oaks.

Fall (September – November)

Identify weak limbs and prepare for seasonal storms. Plan structural work based on the specific tree and site.

Winter (December – February)

Dormancy can make some structural pruning easier to assess, although timing still varies by species and local conditions.
